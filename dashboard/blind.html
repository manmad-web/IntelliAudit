How to review a case

This pilot asks one focused question: is the revenue shown in this income statement supported by the supplied information for the reporting period?

  1. Read the reported statement. It shows amounts as presented. Row numbers let you identify affected lines.
  2. Read the supporting information. Component movements are synthetic summaries for reconciliation. Period-end facts describe conditions to consider. They are not actual invoices, contracts, audit records or independently verified source documents.
  3. Give your initial conclusion. Choose supported, contradicted, insufficient information or unclear. Select relevant evidence and affected rows, explain your reasoning, and identify any missing information.
  4. Add standards detail if you can. The optional section accepts a paragraph citation and a dated source check. Leave these as unknown or not verified when you cannot establish them.
  5. Submit and continue. Your initial answer is locked. After all 20 initial cases are complete, the generated proposal appears automatically. Compare it with your answer and record separate feedback.

Earlier review packets: If your progress panel specifies a delayed repeat, that frozen protocol still applies. Complete its scheduled repeat before opening proposals. The progress panel always shows the requirements for your packet.

Quick glossary

Reported amount
The amount presented in the statement, which you are reviewing.
Component movements
Synthetic positive and negative components supplied to help reconcile an amount; they are not a second financial statement.
Period-end fact
Supplied information about recognition or conditions at the reporting date.
Evidence set
Items that, taken together, support your conclusion. A second set represents an alternative route to the same conclusion.
Governing paragraph
A specific applicable accounting-standard paragraph. A broad topic reference alone does not verify applicability.
Generated proposal
A candidate answer for comparison. It has not been certified as accounting truth.
